# PAIR VETTING — TIC 31315594 vs TIC 31315605 (RA 88, Dec −70 patch)

**Question:** the Codespaces sweep of 2026-11-06 (job 6932389359cb4021) hit two
stars 47″ apart at nearly the same period — TIC 31315594 (P 0.5711 d,
SDE 41.0, depth 11.09%, contamination-flagged) and TIC 31315605 (P 0.5735 d,
SDE 25.8, depth 0.27%). One host + one blend shadow, two independent
signals, or something else?

## VERDICT: BLEND — and the host is neither sweep star

**The signal belongs to TIC 31315574 = WISE J055308.1-700059**, a
catalogued variable (VSX, P = 0.5725948 d) sitting 41.6″ from TIC 31315594
and 83.1″ from TIC 31315605. **Both sweep hits are its shadows:**

- **TIC 31315605 is an honest faint shadow** (~0.5%): the host's eclipses
  diluted in PSF wings 3.96 px away. Its QLP curve and direct aperture
  photometry of its pixels agree.
- **TIC 31315594 carries a corrupted QLP extraction.** Its light curve
  shows the host's eclipses *amplified* to 11–26% — a depth that exists
  nowhere in the actual pixels at its position (own central pixel: 0.2%;
  best aperture anywhere in the frame: ~6%, which is the host's own depth).
  The sweep's SDE-41 headline hit is a pipeline artefact, not photometry.

Decisive stages: **2 (catalogues)** and **5 (difference imaging)**. Stage 4
(folds) alone would have convicted the wrong star — TIC 31315594's curve is
the deepest of the three — which is precisely the failure mode the standing
centroid-first rule exists to catch. Host classification: **EW-type
eclipsing binary**, twin near-equal minima, P_orb = 0.5726 d (minima every
0.2863 d), intrinsic TESS depth ~9–14% after dilution (shallow/partial
eclipses). It is already in VSX via WISE; nothing here is a discovery and
nothing is filable. Both RA 88/−70 hits are booked as **blend-convicted**.

## Stage 3 — What the sweep actually used

`api.py` runs sweeps at **sector_cap = 2** by default, so "persistence
2/2" meant the sweep's entire evidence was **two QLP sectors per star** —
and the two stars' product sets are **completely disjoint**:

- A (31315594): sweep used QLP **sectors 31 + 35**. Full inventory: 20 QLP
  sectors (31, 35–37, 39, 61–65, 67–69, 87, 88, 90, 95–98).
- B (31315605): sweep used QLP **sectors 1 + 2**. Full inventory: 12 QLP
  sectors (1, 2, 4–12, 32).
- C (31315574): 38 filtered products spanning *both* sets — ideal for
  cross-checks in shared sectors.

All vetting below uses QLP light curves (the sweep's own product family)
plus TESSCut FFI pixels, which image all three stars in every sector
regardless of extraction lists.

## Stage 4 — Folds and copy tests

Independent BLS refinement on disjoint 4-sector subsets — **all three
stars peak at the same period, P = 0.572607 d** (VSX: 0.5725948 d,
agreement 0.002%), one shared ephemeris (T0 = BTJD 1354.3284) whose minima
land at phase 0.99 / 0.01 / 0.99 in A / B / C folds spanning 2018–2026.

Full-fold window depths (Min I / Min II, packet estimator, raw
median-normalised flux — reads ≈2× the app's flattened-BLS depths
throughout, e.g. sweep-subset A {31,35} = 24.8% here vs the sweep's
11.09% BLS figure; B {1,2} = 0.54% vs 0.27%):

| A 31315594 (QLP) | 19.85% | 19.03% | 0.272 mag | 20 |
| B 31315605 (QLP) | 0.51% | 0.46% | 0.008 mag | 12 |
| C 31315574 (QLP) | **7.87%** | **7.53%** | **0.100 mag** | 32 |

Per-sector behaviour is the tell. **C's depth is constant at 7.0–8.4% in
every one of its 32 sectors across all eras.** B's is a steady 0.37–0.68%.
**A's swings with the processing batch**: 22.7–26.3% in sectors 31–69,
7.0–20.1% in sectors 87–98 — astrophysics does not respect QLP batch
boundaries; extraction artefacts do.

Copy tests: the three folded profiles are one shape — dip-profile
correlation **A–C r = 0.99995 at zero phase shift, scale A = 2.70 × C**;
B–C r = 0.954, scale 0.062. Per-sector Min-I depths correlate A–C at
r = 0.86 (n = 20 shared sectors, median ratio 2.90); B–C per-sector is
noise-dominated (r = −0.26, n = 12) at a consistent median ratio 0.062.
Wide BLS scans (0.25–10 d) of A and C return **only the harmonic ladder
k × 0.2863 d** — no independent second period exists in any curve.

Fold morphology (host, C): continuous variation with **twin minima of
near-equal depth (7.87% / 7.53%) every 0.2863 d** — the EW/contact-binary
signature, orbital period 0.5726 d. Not a planet-like transit train on any
reading.

## Stage 6 — The QLP paradox and dilution arithmetic

One number refuses the simple story: A's QLP curve (19.85% full fold,
23–26% in era-3 sectors) is *deeper than the host's own curve* (7.87%).
A passive blend can only dilute, never amplify — so A's product was
autopsied directly:

- The anomaly is in the **raw product**: sector-35 SAP_FLUX folds at
  26.19%/24.62%, KSPSAP_FLUX at 25.87%/24.82% (C's file: SAP 8.41%/7.87%).
  QLP's HLSP files publish no aperture extension, so the mask itself
  cannot be inspected.
- A robust grid search over **every aperture** (centres ±3 px around A
  and C, radii 0.75–4 px, median-window depths) on the real FFI pixels
  tops out at **6.2% (s35) / 6.3% (s62)** — the host's own level. No
  aperture photometry of these pixels can produce 26%, or even 11%.
- Conclusion: for this faint target (Tmag 14.49) in this exceptionally
  crowded stamp, QLP's extraction is corrupted by the bright variable
  2 px away, in amplitude as well as in attribution. The exact internal
  mechanism is not readable from the published product; the empirical
  bound is decisive. (Same failure family as TIC 167367406's contratio
  trap, one step worse: there the QLP *depth* was at least real
  photometry of the wrong star; here the depth itself is fabricated.)

Direction-by-direction dilution verdicts:

- **C as host** → B: 0.51% observed ⇒ C supplies ~5% of B's aperture flux
  (wings at 3.96 px) — consistent. → A's *pixels*: 1.0–5.1% by aperture —
  consistent wing shadow of a ~8–11% signal. → A's *QLP curve*: exceeds
  the host's depth — artefact, per the autopsy above.
- **A as host**: ruled out by imaging (centroid on C in 3 sectors / 2
  eras; A's own pixel 0.2%). The fold depths that suggest A are the
  artefact itself.
- **B as host**: ruled out (own pixels ~0.0%; centroid 74–83″ away).
- **TIC 31315592 as host** (0.60 px from A, the dangerous one): ruled out
  — its pixel carries 0.7–1.0%, the centroid sits 31–37″ from it and
  ≤9.2″ from C in every sector.
- **TIC 31315537 as host** (brightest neighbour): ruled out — inside the
  wide frame, carries no variable flux; VSX classifies it type L.
- **Host intrinsic depth**: C's share of its own aperture is ≥0.57 even
  if A+B were captured whole (flux bound), so δ_C ≤ 7.87/0.57 ≈ 13.8%;
  at a realistic ~0.8–0.9 share, δ_C ≈ 9–10% — a shallow-eclipse EW.
